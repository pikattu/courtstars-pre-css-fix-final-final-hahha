NBA LEGACY

League History

Seven decades of milestones, dynasties, and defining moments

🏀
1946
FOUNDING

The NBA Is Born

The Basketball Association of America (BAA) tips off with 11 franchises on November 1, 1946. Three years later it merges with the National Basketball League (NBL) to officially become the NBA — 17 founding teams, two coasts, one mission: make professional basketball matter.

BAA Founding17 Franchises
⏱
1954
RULES

The 24-Second Shot Clock

Syracuse Nationals owner Danny Biasone introduces the 24-second shot clock after a 19–18 disaster of a game proved that stall-ball was killing the sport. The rule transforms basketball overnight — scoring averages jump 13 points per game in a single season, and the fast, continuous game we know today is born.

Rule ChangeDanny Biasone
👑
1960s
DYNASTY

Bill Russell & the Celtic Dynasty

Bill Russell anchors a Boston Celtics team that wins 11 championships in 13 seasons — the most dominant run in North American pro sports. Russell pioneers defensive basketball and wins five MVP awards, proving that protecting the paint and orchestrating team defense is as valuable as any scorer in the league.

11 ChampionshipsBill RussellBoston Celtics
💯
1962
RECORD

Wilt Chamberlain Scores 100

On March 2, 1962 in Hershey, Pennsylvania, Wilt Chamberlain of the Philadelphia Warriors scores 100 points in a single game against the New York Knicks — a record so far beyond reach it may stand for eternity. No TV cameras, no full box score, just a handwritten note confirming what happened. He also averaged 50.4 PPG for the entire season.

Unbroken RecordWilt Chamberlain
3️⃣
1979
RULES

The Three-Point Line Arrives

Adopted from the ABA, the NBA introduces the three-point arc at the start of the 1979–80 season. Ridiculed as a gimmick by purists, it sits dormant for a decade — until Larry Bird and later Reggie Miller weaponize it. By the 2010s, Stephen Curry turns it into the most decisive shot in basketball history.

Rule ChangeABA Legacy
🐐
1984
DRAFT

Michael Jordan Enters the League

The Chicago Bulls select Michael Jordan 3rd overall — behind Hakeem Olajuwon and Sam Bowie. Jordan wins Rookie of the Year, six NBA championships, five MVPs, six Finals MVPs, and transforms basketball into a global phenomenon. The Nike Air Jordan deal he signs that year becomes the most valuable athlete endorsement in history.

Michael JordanChicago Bulls6× Champion
🔥
1980s
RIVALRY

Magic vs. Bird — The Rivalry That Saved the NBA

Magic Johnson and Larry Bird enter the NBA in the same 1979 draft class and immediately become the league's defining rivalry. The Celtics and Lakers meet in the Finals three times in the decade. Their dueling styles — Magic's Showtime fast breaks vs Bird's methodical precision — drive the NBA from near-bankruptcy in the late 1970s to a $1 billion juggernaut by 1990.

Magic JohnsonLarry BirdShowtime Lakers
🥇
1992
GLOBAL

The Dream Team — Basketball Goes Global

For the first time, USA Basketball fields NBA professionals at the Barcelona Olympics. The 1992 Dream Team — Jordan, Magic, Bird, Barkley, Ewing, Pippen, Drexler, Malone, Stockton, Robinson, Mullin, and a college player named Christian Laettner — wins every game by an average of 44 points and exports the NBA to a billion new fans worldwide.

1992 OlympicsBarcelonaGold Medal
🏆
1998
CHAMPIONSHIP

Jordan's Last Shot — The Sixpeat

Game 6 of the 1998 NBA Finals. Jordan steals the ball from Karl Malone, drives the length of the floor, and drains the series-winning jumper over Bryon Russell with 5.2 seconds left. The Bulls' sixth championship in eight years. Jordan retires for the second time, and the basketball world enters the post-Jordan era wondering what comes next.

1998 FinalsChicago Bulls6th Title

2000–02
DYNASTY

Shaq & Kobe's Three-Peat

The Los Angeles Lakers win three consecutive championships behind Shaquille O'Neal's unstoppable interior dominance and Kobe Bryant's relentless competitive fire. Phil Jackson's triangle offense ties it together. O'Neal wins Finals MVP all three times and is at his peak — the most physically dominant player the game has ever seen.

Shaq O'NealKobe BryantLakers Dynasty
📿
2016
COMEBACK

LeBron's Promised Land — The 3-1 Comeback

Down 3–1 to the 73-win Golden State Warriors, LeBron James leads the Cleveland Cavaliers to three straight wins — including back-to-back road wins in Games 5 and 6. In Game 7, LeBron's chase-down block on Andre Iguodala with 1:50 left becomes one of the most replayed moments in Finals history. Cleveland breaks a 52-year championship drought.

LeBron JamesCleveland Cavaliers3-1 Down
🎯
2015–19
REVOLUTION

Curry & the Three-Point Revolution

Stephen Curry breaks the three-point record three times in four seasons, peaking at 402 in 2015–16 — the same year the Warriors finish 73–9, the best regular-season record in NBA history. The Warriors' four championships in eight Finals appearances force every team in the league to reconsider spacing, shooting, and the value of the long two-pointer.

Steph Curry4× Champion73-9 Season
🌀
2019
MOMENT

Kawhi's Bouncing Buzzer-Beater

Game 7 of the second round. Toronto Raptors vs Philadelphia 76ers. With 0.9 seconds left and the score tied, Kawhi Leonard catches an inbound, turns, and heaves a corner four-bouncer that rolls around the rim and falls through. The only walk-off buzzer-beater in NBA Game 7 history. Toronto goes on to win their first championship.

Kawhi LeonardGame 7Toronto Raptors
🧩
2023
CHAMPIONSHIP

Nikola Jokić & Denver's First Title

Nikola Jokić wins his third MVP in four years then leads the Denver Nuggets to their first championship, sweeping Jamal Murray's buzzer-beating heroics into the postseason narrative. Jokić posts 30-14-7 in the Finals sweep-clinching game and is unanimously named Finals MVP — cementing his place as one of the most skilled offensive players the center position has ever produced.

Nikola JokićDenver NuggetsFirst Title
